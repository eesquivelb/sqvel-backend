01 — The Netherlands

Where the idea was born.

The Netherlands is a country that made the sea itself negotiable — flat land reclaimed by patience and engineering, cities built around water instead of against it. That same practicality runs through Dutch style: no excess, no noise, clothes chosen because they work, not because they perform. It's a design culture that produced Droog, Dutch minimalism, and a national wardrobe built on quality basics rather than logos.

SQVEL started as a sketch of that discipline. The idea was simple — nothing decorative, everything considered — and it's still the filter every piece has to pass through before it goes anywhere near a Costa Rican studio or a Portuguese workshop.

02 — Costa Rica

Where the form takes shape.

Costa Rica doesn't chase trends — it never had to. A country that abolished its army to invest in parks and schools built a culture around what actually matters: living well, close to the land, without pretense. That same instinct shows up in how people dress here — relaxed, unhurried, built for a body in motion rather than a body on display. It's a rare kind of fashion sense: confident enough to be simple.

That's the mindset SQVEL borrows for the design stage. Every silhouette, cut, and proportion is worked out here — slower, by hand, close to the material before it becomes a pattern. Pair that ease with Dutch precision and Portuguese craft, and what comes out the other end is a piece that feels effortless to wear and impossible to fake.

03 — Portugal

Where it's actually made.

Portugal has quietly become one of Europe's most respected garment-making countries — the same ateliers responsible for pieces sold under some of fashion's biggest names sit in Porto and the north, running on generations of tailoring knowledge most brands never mention out loud. It's a culture that treats craft as inheritance, not a job title, and it shows in how precisely a Portuguese-made seam holds up over years of actual wear.

That's what closes the loop for SQVEL. A Dutch idea, shaped by Costa Rican hands, finished by Portuguese ones — small workshops, real tailoring, construction that survives being worn, not just photographed. Three very different design cultures, each contributing the thing they're actually best at, is exactly why a SQVEL piece feels like more than the sum of where it came from.
